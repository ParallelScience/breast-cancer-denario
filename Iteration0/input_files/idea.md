Title: Utility-Optimized Calibration of Multicollinearity-Robust 'Worst' Feature Models for Breast Cancer Diagnosis

This research aims to develop a robust framework for generating clinically-actionable, calibrated probabilistic breast cancer diagnoses at high specificity. We will address inherent multicollinearity by employing targeted dimensionality reduction (e.g., PCA) specifically on the 'worst' statistics of highly correlated nuclear morphology feature groups (e.g., `worst radius`, `worst perimeter`, `worst area`, and `worst compactness`, `worst concavity`, `worst concave points`), creating orthogonal, interpretable features that capture tail severity. Subsequently, we will systematically optimize and evaluate standard post-hoc calibration strategies for their impact on sensitivity across a spectrum of simulated clinical utility functions and high-specificity operating points, reflecting varying false negative/positive cost asymmetries. Performance will be rigorously quantified using nested cross-validation with confidence intervals, prioritizing reliable risk stratification for malignant cases and identifying a robust pipeline that demonstrably enhances decision-making utility under clinically relevant cost profiles.
