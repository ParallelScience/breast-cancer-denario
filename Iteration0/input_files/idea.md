**Title**: Inferring Latent Intra-Tumor Morphological Distributions for Malignancy Characterization

**Description**: This research aims to uncover novel diagnostic signals by inferring the latent within-tumor distributions of nuclear morphology, leveraging the `mean`, `error`, and `worst` statistics as a crude 3-moment sketch for each of the ten base measurements. We propose to fit parametric or semi-parametric distributions (e.g., skewed-normal, mixture models) to these three summary statistics for each FNA sample, thereby reconstructing a more complete representation of intra-tumor heterogeneity. The derived parameters of these inferred distributions (e.g., skewness, kurtosis, or specific tail characteristics beyond simple `worst` values) will serve as novel, high-order features. We hypothesize that malignant samples will exhibit distinct patterns in these latent distributional parameters, reflecting fundamental cellular dysregulation and providing superior discrimination compared to raw summary statistics, especially for sensitivity at high specificity. Rigorous evaluation will employ nested cross-validation with confidence intervals, explicitly addressing multicollinearity's impact on parameter interpretation.
